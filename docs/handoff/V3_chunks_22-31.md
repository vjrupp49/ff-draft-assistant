## Arc 1: Sequencing correctness (Chunks 22–27)

**Chunk 22 — Live dry run #3.** Validated Chunks 20–21 together. Opponent model accuracy held steady (confirming Chunk 21's fix), but surfaced a new problem: real reaches of 90+ picks early on players like Alvin Kamara and James Conner, alongside a recurring WR shortage / TE glut. The app itself flagged these picks as "statistical near-ties" — question became whether the ties were genuine or a symptom of something broken.

**Chunk 23 — Diagnostic: why doesn't MCTS defer safe players?** Instrumented the real pick-59 decision (Kamara vs. McLaurin) directly. Found and corrected a real measurement bug along the way: `picks_until_next_turn()` returns 0 while on the clock, so the original "92-pick margin" was miscalculated (corrected to 88.8, anchored to the actual next turn). Refuted the "rollout depth too shallow" hypothesis — the tree's ~23-pick horizon doesn't need to see all the way to a safe player's ADP, because the opponent model already extrapolates safety correctly within that window. Real finding: Kamara's backfill-later is ~100% free; McLaurin's is only ~40% free (comparable WRs get taken instead). That's a genuine small edge toward taking McLaurin now, but it's smaller than Monte Carlo noise (diff/SE = 0.63) — a real, structural near-tie, not underpowered simulation. Proposed (not built): an ADP-margin tie-break applied only among statistically-tied candidates.

**Chunk 24 — Verify + build the tie-break.** Verification at two more reach points (Conner/pick 79, Brian Thomas/pick 39) showed the Chunk 23 mechanism does NOT generalize — pick 79 was actually NOT a near-tie once fairly measured (z=2.22, z=3.11), just an artifact of iterations being diluted across up to 8 root candidates. Proceeded anyway since the tie-break's target behavior (prefer the more at-risk tied option) still held directionally in most cases. Built `_apply_adp_margin_tie_break`: fires only on candidates MCTS already flags `within_noise_of_leader`, promotes whichever has the smallest real-ADP margin to the user's actual next turn. No-ADP-match candidates can never win by default. Validated, regression-tested, negative-controlled.

**Chunk 25 — Diagnostic: is Chunk 24's fix actually well-justified?** User pushback: Chunk 24 shipped despite finding pick 79 wasn't a real tie at all — is "budget dilution" (not genuine indifference) explaining some flagged ties, and would fair iteration allocation resolve them without needing the ADP tie-break at all? Answer: yes, pervasively. 13 of 15 real picks had `within_noise_of_leader` groups; production caps each tied candidate at ~19–30 effective iterations (vs. ~3,000 in a forced-root test). Fair-budgeted retest of 6 traced picks: every group shed its weakest member, but only 2 of 6 fully resolved to a single winner — the rest retain a genuine tied core even at 20x budget. Critical finding: ADP-margin tie-break agreement with the *true* (fairly-measured) winner was 0/2 on the cleanly-resolved cases — tighter ADP margin is not a reliable proxy for higher value. Found a concrete production bug: at pick 122, the tie-break promoted Jeudy over Njoku purely because Jeudy had ADP data and Njoku didn't, even though Jeudy was objectively the worst option (z=18.31) — a default-win, not a merit-win, and a bug pattern specific to TE-heavy decisions (TEs disproportionately lack ADP matches).

**Chunk 26 — Build: reorder decision logic, fix the pick-122 bug.** Implemented `_adaptively_resolve_tie`: any tied group gets a focused re-search (up to 600 iterations in batches, stopping early if resolved) before any tie-break applies. A confident winner from this step is trusted directly, never cross-checked against ADP (per Chunk 25's finding that they can disagree). Only a group still tied after this falls back to the ADP tie-break — and the pick-122 default-win bug is fixed structurally, since a bad no-ADP-match candidate now gets eliminated by adaptive resolution before the ADP criterion ever sees the group. Bonus find during validation: a real, second instance of the Chunk 15 determinism bug class — `FANTASY_POSITIONS`/`SUPER_FLEX_ELIGIBLE` were Python `set` literals whose hash-randomized iteration order fed into a stable sort, silently changing recommendations across process restarts independent of any seed. Fixed (tuples instead of sets). Two pre-existing tests updated with justification (not silently patched) after confirming adaptive resolution reveals genuinely different — not incorrectly different — best picks at those decision points.

**Chunk 27 — Verification-only close-out.** Confirmed the 600-iteration cap has real margin where ties are resolvable, while 2 of 6 seed-instances never resolve even at 2x the cap — correctly identified as genuine irreducible ties, not a cap-sizing failure. Confirmed full-pipeline worst-case latency (7.89s max, inside the two-tier recompute path) is safe for live draft pace. Swept the whole codebase for the same set-literal bug class — found and confirmed no other instances. Confirmed the corrected pick-14 test was factually valid before Chunk 26 and remains valid after (adaptive resolution doesn't change the underlying reward landscape, only reveals what was already true with more precision). **Chunks 13–27 close the sequencing/decision-logic correctness chapter.**

## Arc 2: Data quality investigation (Chunks 28–31, in progress)

Triggered by user concern, independent of the sequencing arc: is the system reaching for players like Kamara/Conner because it has genuine league-specific insight (TE premium, SUPERFLEX), or because its underlying player valuations are simply less accurate than Sleeper's own rankings? Explicitly scoped as upstream of and separate from the sequencing work — bad projections would undermine even a perfectly-sequenced decision engine.

**Chunk 28 — Diagnostic: where does the divergence from Sleeper actually enter?** Corrected the task's own premise: there is no FantasyPros/ESPN source anywhere in the codebase (confirmed still true from Chunk 16) — the real pipeline is nfl_data_py historical stats (primary) + FFC ADP (secondary, only for rookies and detected team-changers). Major finding: **nfl_data_py's most recent usable season was 2024** — a full real 2025 NFL season had been played and was completely invisible to the system, while the ADP cache was genuinely current. Decomposed Kelce's TE-dominant valuation directly: TE premium scoring explains only ~15–18% of his projection edge over market; the remaining ~82–85% is stale 2024 volume the real market had already marked down. Verdict: **not** "projections were fine, it was purely sequencing" — a real, active input-quality problem, concentrated in established/team-unchanged veterans who get zero forward-looking correction.

**Chunk 29 — Diagnostic: root-cause the staleness, decompose Kamara/Conner, sanity-check the existing blend.** Root-caused the 2024 ceiling: nfl_data_py's underlying `player_stats` release was abandoned by its maintainers in favor of a new `stats_player` release (rebuilt schema, actively updated through the current date) — nfl_data_py (PyPI, unmaintained) was simply never pointed at it. Confirmed via the maintainers' own GitHub issues that they now recommend migrating away entirely. This is a fixable package/pipeline problem, not a "the season hasn't happened yet" limitation. Decomposed Kamara/Conner directly using already-working endpoints (`import_injuries`, `import_snap_counts`, which don't have the same staleness problem): both had genuine, discrete 2025 season-ending injuries — Kamara's snap share collapsed from ~80% to as low as 14% by week 12; Conner was out weeks 11–12. Confirmed as pure staleness, not a structural blind spot fresher data wouldn't fix. Sanity-checked the existing 65/35 team-changed ADP blend against 3 externally-corroborated real trades — the math itself is sound. But found a real trigger bug: `team_changed()` does plain string equality, and nfl_data_py uses `"LA"` for the Rams while Sleeper uses `"LAR"` — a false-positive team-change flag firing on ~240 players (~24% of the pool), confirmed on at least 3 players still on their actual team (Nacua, Stafford, Kyren Williams).

**Chunk 30 — Build: migrate off dead nfl_data_py stats source.** Migrated to a direct parquet fetch against nflverse's current, actively-maintained release (chosen over `nflreadpy`, which is marked experimental and would add 6 new dependencies) — zero new dependencies required. Remapped 4 renamed schema columns; audited the full codebase and confirmed only `projections.py` consumes them. Updated the recency-weighting window from [2024, 2023, 2022] to [2025, 2024, 2023]. Confirmed real numbers moved as expected and traced to real cause: Kamara −32%, Conner −45%, both matching their actual 2025 games-played collapse from `import_injuries`/`import_snap_counts` data. Regression suite: 11 failures, individually triaged — 9 were stale-fixture artifacts of the old data era (re-verified against real ADP/context before repinning, not bulk-updated) and 2 were a genuine, real re-emergence of the WR-shortage/TE-glut pattern, root-caused to a real elite 2025 TE season (Trey McBride) now visible for the first time — explicitly deferred and marked `xfail(strict=True)` with documentation rather than papered over. Negative-controlled (reverted code + deleted disk cache to force genuine rebuild — confirmed failure).

**Chunk 31 — Scoped, not yet run.** LA/LAR team-abbreviation fix (isolated from the stats migration deliberately, to keep negative controls clean), a live Sleeper dry run to check whether the original Chunk 22 over-reach pattern is resolved now that both staleness and the trigger bug are addressed, and specific attention to whether the Chunk 30 TE-glut `xfail` is a McBride-specific outlier or a broader tier effect. Preceded by a quick QB/WR sanity check of the Chunk 30 migration (only RB/TE were spot-checked there, since those were the motivating cases) before proceeding to the main task. **Report pending.**
